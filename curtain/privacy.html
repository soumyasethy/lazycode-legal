Curtain privacy policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026 · App: Curtain (ai.lazycode.curtain) · Developer: Lazycode (Soumya Ranjan Sethy) · Data we hold when you sign in: name, email address and account ID, plus your Google Play order ID if you have Pro · Contact: soumya@lazycode.ai · Website: lazycode.ai/curtain

Curtain hides your screen from people next to you while you type. When your keyboard opens, a curtain covers everything above it. To do that, Curtain uses an Android accessibility service that reads where the keyboard is, which app is open and whether the focused field is a password field. It never reads what you type.

What the accessibility service reads

  • The keyboard's position on screen, to know where the curtain stops and when the keyboard opens or closes.
  • The name of the app in front (its package name), to remember your pill position per app and to apply your auto-curtain and skip lists.
  • The password-field flag of the focused field, so a password or PIN field can curtain itself. For slit mode, which shows only the line you are typing in, it also reads the focused field's size and position.

Curtain never reads or stores the text you type, the contents of any field, or what is on your screen. The service is used for these purposes only. Before you switch it on, the app shows a disclosure that explains the same in plain words, and you can switch it off at any time in Android's Accessibility settings.

What stays on your phone

Your settings (curtain colour and opacity, pill look and position, the apps you add to the auto-curtain and skip lists, your schedule) are saved in the app's private storage on your device. Uninstalling Curtain deletes them. The list of apps you choose is never sent to us.

Usage and crash data

Curtain uses Google Firebase Analytics, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Performance Monitoring to see where setup gets stuck, fix crashes and keep the app fast. This data is processed by Google under Firebase's privacy terms.

  • Usage events: setup steps shown or skipped, whether a permission was granted, when the curtain is armed or falls (mode and what triggered it, with a duration range rather than exact times), whether the pill was moved, how quickly the pill appeared, settings that changed, and paywall views, whether a setup permission was granted and how long each setup screen was open, which screens you visit inside Curtain, whether the curtain has fallen for the first time, and once a day rounded counts (none, 1 to 4, 5 to 19, 20 or more) of how often the keyboard opened, the pill appeared and the curtain fell, never which app.
  • Crash and error reports: stack traces and non-fatal errors such as the overlay failing to appear or the accessibility service disconnecting, with your phone brand and model, Android version, the keyboard family (for example Gboard, Samsung or SwiftKey), the curtain mode and whether touch-through is on. Problems that stop the pill or the curtain appearing are also counted as usage events, at most once a day each.
  • Performance data: how long the app takes to start and draw its screens, with your phone brand, model and Android version. No network timing is collected.
  • Identifiers: Firebase creates an app-instance identifier so events and crashes from one install can be grouped, and reports say whether they come from a Play install or a test build. If you sign in, reports also carry a one-way code made from your account ID, not from your name or email. We can match it to your account, for example to contact you about a crash. Curtain also sets plain labels on your install, such as the keyboard family (for example Gboard or Samsung), which mode and switches you use, and whether you are on the free plan, a trial or Pro, so we can see which setups work.

Never collected: what you type, the contents of fields, what is on your screen, or which apps you pick.

This is on by default, except in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, where it stays off until you agree when Curtain asks once. One switch controls all three, in Settings under Share usage and crash data. To ask us to delete data already collected from your install, follow these steps.

Signing in (optional)

You can use Curtain without an account, and every feature works the same signed out.

If you choose Continue with Google, Google tells us your name and email address, held in Firebase Authentication with the account's creation and last sign-in time. We use them to run your account, for support, to contact you about problems such as crashes, for internal reporting and analysis, and for email from Lazycode as described below. Our own systems can read them for these purposes. We never sell them. Curtain's settings, app lists and anything on your screen are never part of your account.

Email: outside the EEA, UK and Switzerland, signing in means you agree we may email you about your account and with news from Lazycode, and the sign-in screen says so. Inside those regions, or where we cannot tell, Curtain asks and sends nothing without a Yes. Change it any time in Home, Account. The choice, its basis, time and policy version are stored in Firestore in a record only your account can read or write through the app.

Pro: buying Pro never needs an account. If you are signed in when you buy, or sign in later, we store the product, the time it was linked and your Google Play order ID in that same private record, so Pro stays linked to you. A purchase made while signed in also carries the one-way code described above. We never see your payment details.

Deleting: Home, Account, Delete account, or email soumya@lazycode.ai. Signing out deletes nothing.

Not a screenshot or camera blocker

Curtain is software that hides your screen from a glance. It does not block screenshots, screen recordings or a camera pointed at your phone.

Ads and purchases

Curtain shows no ads. If it offers an optional Pro unlock, payment is handled entirely by Google Play under Google's privacy policy. We never see your payment details.

Children

Curtain is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.
